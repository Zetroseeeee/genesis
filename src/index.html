Where the world comes from

The picture of the Earth. NASA's Blue Marble Next Generation (R. Stöckli, E. Vermote, N. Saleous, R. Simmon and D. Herring, NASA Earth Observatory, 2005): the land as the MODIS instrument saw it through 2004, at 500 m. Each hemisphere is shown in its summer; the towns of our own day are painted over with the country round them.

Winter. Where snow lies and for how long is read from the twelve months of the same Blue Marble. Where the sea freezes and for how long is from the Sea Ice Index, Version 4 (F. Fetterer, K. Knowles, W. N. Meier, M. Savoie and A. K. Windnagel; National Snow and Ice Data Center, Boulder, Colorado; doi:10.7265/a98x-0f50): the usual year of its first ten, 1979 to 1988.

The sky. The stars are those of the Bright Star Catalogue, 5th revised edition (D. Hoffleit and W. H. Warren Jr., 1991), the Hipparcos Catalogue (ESA, 1997, SP-1200) and the Tycho-2 Catalogue (E. Høg and others, 2000), as NASA's High Energy Astrophysics Science Archive Research Center keeps them: every star to the tenth magnitude, where it stood in the year 2000, turned back to where it stood in the game's year. The Milky Way is from Deep Star Maps 2020 and the Moon from the CGI Moon Kit, both of NASA/Goddard Space Flight Center Scientific Visualization Studio (the Milky Way from Gaia DR2: ESA/Gaia/DPAC; the Moon from the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter's camera and laser altimeter). The clouds are the Blue Marble's (R. Stöckli, NASA Earth Observatory): those the Earth had in the last days of July 2001.
